The Milky Way

New research suggests our galactic home spans a distance of 200,000 light-years

Some other interesting facts:

  • Age:The Milky Way is around 13.6 billion years old.
  • Size: The Milky Way is a spiral galaxy that's about 100,000 light-years across.
  • Stars: The Milky Way contains over 200 billion stars, including our Sun.
  • Rotation: The Milky Way rotates every 250 million years.
  • Globular clusters: The Milky Way is surrounded by over 150 globular clusters, which are groups of stars that orbit the galactic center.
  • Supernovae: The Milky Way is constantly losing stars through supernovae, but it also produces new stars at a rate of about seven per year.
  • Name: The Milky Way's name comes from the ancient Greeks, who called the white streak of stars in the night sky a "river of milk".
  • Part of a supercluster: The Milky Way is part of the Virgo Supercluster, which is a group of galaxies.
  • Part of the Local Group: The Milky Way is part of the Local Group, which includes the Milky Way and its nearby surroundings.
  • Visible stars: From any point on Earth, you can only see about 2,500 stars in the Milky Way.
